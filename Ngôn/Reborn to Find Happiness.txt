Reborn to Find Happiness

I had been locked away like a caged bird for ten full years.

By the time I drew my final breath, he still had never given me a real title or a proper wedding.

When my eyes snapped open once more, I was stunned to realize I had returned to a time ten years in the past.

Determined not to repeat the tragedy of my previous life, the moment I opened my eyes, I immediately set out to find the man who would later become his bitter, arch-nemesis.

At this point in time, he was nothing more than a dirt-poor auto mechanic.

I took him by the hand, set a bank card firmly on the table, and said, "Marry me. From now on, my entire salary belongs to you."

He gritted his teeth and grinned, a sly glint in his eyes. "Three grand a month? If you're fine with that, then we're getting married."

-----

Chapter 1

And just like that, I proposed to Jax.

The man kept a cigarette dangling from his lips, squinting at me through the thin veil of smoke. "What do you even like about me?"

I racked my brain for a brief moment before blurting out, "You're good-looking."

"Ha."

His low scoff made my face burn with heat. Gathering every ounce of my courage, I pressed on, "So... is that a yes?"

Jax fell silent. His intense gaze bored into me, as though he had already seen right through every petty calculation in my heart.

I bit my lip hard and looked straight back into his eyes, hoping he could sense my complete sincerity.

After a long pause, the corner of his lips tugged upward into a faint smirk. "A wife walking right up to my doorstep? I'd be an idiot to turn that down."

Just like that, I actually got married.

It was only the second day after my rebirth.

Holding the bright red marriage certificate in my hands, my mind still felt completely hazy, as if I were lost in a dream.

In my past life, I had indeed met Jax once.

It was during the seventh year of my captivity under Julian.

I had managed to find a rare opportunity to escape that dark, suffocating mansion, without a single penny in my pockets.

On a completely unfamiliar street, I accidentally bumped into Jax.

By then, he was already an immensely successful businessman. Tailored suits and an icy, commanding aura made him seem utterly intimidating and unreachable.

Exhausted and desperate, I begged him for help.

Jax arranged a temporary place for me to stay and even bought me a plane ticket to leave the city.

Unfortunately, I still failed to escape Julian's relentless grasp in the end.

Not wanting to reopen those painful wounds of the past, I lifted my head to look at Jax. "Where are we going now?"

"Taking you home."

I blinked in confusion. "Aren't we... already husband and wife now?"

"So?"

"I mean... aren't you going to bring me back to your place?"

Jax's place was located inside an unfinished residential building, where the ceilings hadn't even been poured yet.

All around us echoed the harsh thuds of hammers and the busy footsteps of construction workers.

"I used to haul bricks around here, so I know the crew chief pretty well," Jax explained on his own initiative, noticing my wide-eyed shock. "I'm staying here temporarily while waiting for the building to be handed over. Saves on rent."

The room bore a stark, unfinished industrial vibe.

There was no valuable furniture to speak of, aside from a single folding cot.

The bathroom didn't even have a toilet installed, and the shower area consisted of nothing more than a bare, exposed water pipe.

Jax turned back to look at me. "Regretting it already? If you want to run, there's still time."

"I don't regret it."

I set my luggage aside and asked hesitantly, "Are both of us sleeping here tonight? This bed looks a little small..."

Jax narrowed his eyes, uttering a slow, deep response. "It's just right."

As night fell, I lay on his folding cot.

Jax laid two sheets of old newspaper on the floor and lay down there instead.

The room had no curtains. Through the bare window, fractured moonlight streamed in, illuminating the quiet corner of the room.

"Jax, are you asleep?"

"Yeah."

Fearing he would catch a cold lying on the floor, I shifted my body toward the edge of the cot. "Why don't... you come up and sleep here with me?"

The bed was small, but if we squeezed close together, there was still enough room.

A few seconds ticked by before Jax's raspy, thick voice echoed. "If I come up there, don't expect to get any sleep tonight."

I gripped the corner of the blanket tightly. "I don't mind."

Hearing those words, Jax moved like a predator receiving a direct command, instantly rolling over and pressing down on top of me.

Taken completely off guard, I instinctively turned my head to the side.

The heavy weight on me suddenly eased. Through the dim moonlight, I caught a fleeting, mocking smirk playing on his lips.

"Put your money where your mouth is. I'm a honest guy, real easy to fool."

He let go, preparing to climb back down to his newspaper blanket on the floor.

Seeing him leave, I frantically reached out and grabbed the hem of his shirt.

My fingers slipped past the fabric, accidentally touching his warm, bare skin.

Jax's body stiffened abruptly, his dark eyes instantly turning ominously intense.

I bit my lip hard. "I really meant it."

Terrified he might walk away again, I mustered the nerve to lightly scratch his waist twice.

Jax's gaze darkened to a frightening degree. "Now you've officially run out of ways to back down."

Unlike the polished, gym-sculpted muscles Julian had, Jax's body was lean, sinewy, and naturally tanned, forged through years of backbreaking labor.

Every single abdominal muscle was firm and packed with raw power.

In the end, I couldn't bear it anymore and burst into uncontrollable sobs, forcing him to finally stop.

He bent down awkwardly, kissing away the tears on my face, his voice carrying a mix of heartache and slight dissatisfaction. "Fragile."

My body was aching all over, and instead of getting comforting words, I was getting scolded. A wave of petty resentment washed over me, and I turned my back on him, refusing to look at him any longer.

The man made no attempt to coax me either, quietly stepping off the cot and disappearing somewhere.

I pulled the blanket tightly over my head, feeling more aggrieved the more I thought about it.

In my past life, I had been caged like a bird by Julian for ten whole years, gaining freedom only upon my death.

I had assumed heaven was showing mercy by granting me a second chance to start over.

Who could have guessed that Jax would turn out to be such an insensitive brute as well?

Chapter 2

As I was quietly whimpering, a warm towel was suddenly laid gently over my face.

Jax had returned at some point, holding a basin of warm water and a small towel in his hands.

"Crying?"

I stubbornly denied it. "No, I'm not."

Jax wiped my face roughly a few times before turning back to wring out the cloth.

"What are you doing?"

"There's no hot water installed in the house yet, so you can't take a proper shower. Wiping down like this will make you feel a bit better."

My body was indeed feeling very sticky and uncomfortable.

Setting aside my petty mood, I flushed and reached out to snatch the towel away. "Alright, let me do it myself."

"Sit still."

Ignoring my weak resistance, Jax pulled the blanket aside and gently wiped me clean, bit by bit.

A moment later, his deep, low voice sounded beside my ear once more.

"Don't be mad anymore. I'll be gentler next time."

His tone carried an awkwardness that was completely different from his usual demeanor.

A searing warmth spread rapidly across my face. Unable to control the corners of my mouth from tugging upward, I hurriedly pulled the blanket over my head.

When I woke up the next morning, Jax was already fully dressed.

On the small table sat two steaming hot steamed buns that he must have woken up early to buy.

"I boiled some hot water. Clean yourself up, and I'll take you to work."

"There's no need. I can go by myself."

Jax glanced at me sideways. "This construction site is packed with workers. It's not safe for a girl to walk out alone."

He then added a quick instruction, "Call me when you get off work tonight, and I'll come pick you up."

I casually freshened up, only then noticing the bank card still resting quietly on the table.

It was the very card I had used to propose to him yesterday.

"You forgot to take this card."

Jax shifted his gaze over. "You keep it. I haven't stooped to the point where I need to spend a woman's money."

Overthinking things as usual, even though he was undeniably dirt-poor.

Since he refused to take it, I didn't bother arguing.

In my heart, I began mentally calculating whether I could use this money to get a different place to live.

Even if Jax refused to move to another apartment, we could at least buy a much more comfortable bed.

Though there was no gold-dusted mansion like in my past life, simply seeing Jax brought an extraordinary sense of peace and security to my heart.

That cheerful mood lasted all the way until I arrived at the school.

The moment I stepped into the faculty office, my coworker Violet hurried over with a face full of mystery. "Chloe, I've got great news for you!"

She lowered her voice. "My cousin has his eyes on you!"

I blinked in complete bewilderment. "Huh?"

Violet pulled me into a quiet corner. "My cousin is no ordinary guy, you know. He's handsome, has a great personality, and even graduated from Cambridge. His dad is a well-known tycoon, and countless rich socialites try to chase him down, but he doesn't even glance their way. Yet yesterday, he suddenly asked me to play matchmaker for the two of you!"

Violet's eyes sparkled with excitement. "This is your chance to completely turn your life around, girl!"

Graduated from Cambridge.

His father was a tycoon.

A terrifyingly familiar name instantly flashed across my mind.

The blood drained from my face in an instant, leaving me deathly pale.

Stifling the chaotic emotions surging within me, I forced myself to respond in the most nonchalant tone possible. "Please apologize to your cousin for me. I don't have that kind of luck—I'm already married."

Violet's eyes widened in utter disbelief. "We've been colleagues for a whole year! You didn't even have a boyfriend, so how on earth did you suddenly get married out of nowhere?"

"It's true. We've already registered the marriage, we just haven't held the ceremony yet. When we do, I'll definitely send you an invitation."

I managed a forced smile. "I still have two classes to teach, so I need to head out first."

With that, I hurried out the door.

Only after stepping out of the office did every ounce of strength drain from my body, leaving me utterly weak.

Julian.

In my past life, he clearly hadn't appeared at this point in time.

Why was he coming into my life so much earlier this time around?

How did he even find out that Violet and I worked at the same elementary school?

My hands and feet turned ice-cold.

I tried to reassure myself that in this life, I was already someone's wife, and I had Jax by my side.

I would absolutely never walk down that dark, disastrous path again.

Julian's sudden appearance left my mind in complete turmoil for the rest of the day.

I couldn't focus on my work at all, and my right eyelid kept twitching non-stop.

A vague, ominous feeling told me something terrible was about to happen.

Sure enough, the moment I stepped out of the school gates after work, I saw a sleek black luxury car parked right outside.

A man stepped out of the vehicle, holding a lavish bouquet of bright red roses in his hands.

"Miss Crawford."

He offered a polite smile as he introduced himself. "My apologies for disturbing you. I'm Julian, Violet's cousin."

The school gate was crowded with passing students and parents at that hour.

The bouquet of roses in Julian's hands was far too ostentatious, causing many curious onlookers to turn their heads and stare.

A few colleagues recognized me and immediately started clapping and cheering, urging me to accept the flowers.

My face turned pale as ash. "What do you think you're doing?"

"Don't be nervous, I just want to get to know you." The corner of Julian's mouth tugged into a slight smirk. "The last time I accidentally ran into you while waiting for Violet, I was deeply impressed. Please don't be uncomfortable that I came looking for you today; let's just consider this a friendly meeting."

Even though Julian concealed it exceptionally well, I still caught a glimpse of fleeting madness lurking deep within his eyes.

Just as I was caught in a panic, unsure of how to handle the situation, I suddenly spotted Jax standing near the corner of the gate.

He was slouched casually over an old motorcycle, a cigarette hanging from his mouth, looking over in our direction with a mocking glint in his eyes.

Feeling as though an insurmountable obstacle had been swept away, I immediately dashed toward him.

The familiar scent of motor oil clinging to Jax slowly steadied my racing heart.

I turned back toward Julian and spoke firmly. "I made it very clear to Violet. I'm already married."

Julian froze in surprise, the polite smile on his lips stiffening completely.

A dark, resentful glare flickered across his eyes as he directed his gaze toward Jax.

Though he retracted the expression in a split second, I caught that subtle moment with sharp precision.

Resentment?

This was clearly the very first time the two of them had ever met, so why would he look at him like that?

Chapter 3

Regaining his composure, Julian offered a natural smile. "It seems I was too abrupt and failed to properly look into your marital status, Miss Crawford. Since I've already bought these flowers, please accept them as a wedding gift to you both."

He stepped forward to hand over the bouquet. "Gifting flowers to a beautiful lady—surely your husband wouldn't mind, right?"

Before I could even open my mouth, Jax let out a quiet, mocking laugh.

"Who says I don't mind?"

He darted a cool glance at Julian. "I happen to be pretty narrow-minded. I don't like watching my wife accept gifts from other men."

With that, Jax casually shoved a bright pink helmet onto my head.

"Put it on."

Looking at the brand-new helmet, my mood instantly brightened up completely.

I couldn't help asking, "Did you buy this specifically for me?"

"Dug it out of the trash heap."

I pouted. "Then next time, dig up a green one. I like that color better."

Jax raised an eyebrow, making a mock motion to snatch the helmet back.

I hurriedly held tightly onto the helmet and tapped my head twice. "Does it look good?"

A gentle, doting look softened Jax's eyes. He reached out, pulled the visor down, and muttered, "Dummy."

By the time we looked back, Julian had already quietly left.

After that incident, the entire school knew that my husband was nothing more than a simple auto mechanic.

During the break, I overheard a few teachers huddled together, gossiping in hushed tones.

"What's the point of being pretty? In the end, she still ended up with a worthless husband."

"Didn't you guys see that beat-up motorcycle of his? It makes as much noise as a tractor."

The entire group burst into laughter at the remark.

Seeing me walk in, someone deliberately teased, "Chloe, which auto repair shop does your husband work at? My husband just bought a Mercedes; I'll be sure to swing by to support his business whenever it needs maintenance."

"Sure, I'll send you the address in a bit."

Violet couldn't stand it any longer. She dragged me out of the office by my arm. "What on earth is wrong with your head? People are mocking you to your face, and you're just standing there taking it?"

"I'm telling you the truth. You're pretty and you have a stable job, so why on earth did you throw yourself into..."

She paused briefly before continuing, "Frankly speaking, you just graduated, so it's easy for you to be fooled by a few sweet words. You think love is everything now, but you'll definitely regret it down the road. Why don't you reconsider my cousin? He said he doesn't mind that you've been married..."

"Violet, that's a nice bag you have."

I cut her off, my eyes resting on the expensive designer bag sitting on her desk.

"Is it new?"

Violet froze, her face flushing bright red. "Yeah."

Violet was just an ordinary teacher. Where that pricey luxury bag came from and what purpose it served were things I understood all too clearly.

I didn't bother exposing her. "My husband and I get along very well. Our private matters aren't any outsider's concern. Please don't speak about him like that again in the future—I don't like hearing it."

When work ended, Jax came to pick me up, but he arrived on foot.

"Where's your motorcycle?"

"Sold it."

Jax offered no further explanation, simply curving his lips into a faint smile. "Let's go."

He took my hand and pulled me along. The strange thing was, this time he didn't take our usual route home.

After walking a few hundred yards, he stopped in front of an old residential complex located near my school.

"Let's go up to the third floor and take a look."

A wave of suspicion swelled in my heart, yet I hardly dared to believe what it implied.

I tiptoed up to the third floor, where the door to the center apartment stood wide open.

Stepping inside, a cozy two-bedroom apartment caught my eye.

Though it wasn't overly spacious, the atmosphere felt exceptionally warm and welcoming.

Sky-blue curtains fluttered gently in the light breeze.

The evening sun poured through the window, bathing the entire room in a brilliant golden glow.

He had remembered that I liked the color blue.

An indescribable surge of emotion welled up in my chest.

Having lived through two lifespans, I had stayed in a luxurious mansion and inhabited a dilapidated, bare-bones structure, yet only in this very moment did my heart feel completely filled with pure happiness.

"Did you sell your motorcycle just to rent this place?"

The area around the school consisted entirely of apartments rented out to teachers; though the building was older, the rent was by no means cheap.

Jax's savings probably weren't enough to cover both the security deposit and three months' rent upfront.

Jax brushed off the topic of selling his bike, merely replying, "It's close to your school, so it's convenient for your commute. Besides, the other place doesn't have hot water. How could a delicate lady like you take that?"

Before he could finish his sentence, I threw myself forward and wrapped my arms tightly around his waist.

My sudden gesture caught Jax completely off guard.

He froze for a brief moment before quickly opening his arms wide, wrapping them securely around my back from behind.

"Alright, now's not the time to cry. Save those tears for tonight so you can show me properly, okay?"

He really was utterly shameless!

Chapter 4

That very night.

Jax truly made me cry out loud. The man wrapped his arms tightly around me, pressing his lips against the corner of my mouth as he teased, "My wife was right—a double bed really is much better to lie on than a single cot. Guess I was just too slow to catch on."

I... when had I ever said such a thing!

Despite feeling wronged, I ultimately couldn't bring myself to kick him off the bed.

The next day was the weekend, and Jax and I had agreed to return to the unfinished building to clear out the remaining belongings.

However, by late afternoon, there was still no sign of him anywhere.

"I'll probably be back a bit late today; a little issue popped up down at the shop," Jax took a quick moment to call and inform me.

Without thinking much of it, I replied, "Then I'll just head over by myself to grab our stuff."

In truth, there wasn't much to pack aside from a small suitcase.

Jax's end of the line sounded extremely noisy. Unable to hear whatever else he said clearly, I simply hung up the phone.

Previously, Jax had strictly forbidden me from going there alone because the area was filled with construction workers, and he worried about my safety.

However, it was broad daylight right now. If I just popped in to grab our things and left immediately, nothing bad could possibly happen.

To my utter surprise, the second I unlocked the door and stepped into the room, three or four male workers quietly slipped in right behind me.

"All by yourself here, little girl?"

My heart skipped a terrifying beat. "Who are you people? Get out right now!"

"We work here, so why should we leave?"

The ringleader let out a sneering laugh. "We've been keeping an eye on you and that guy for a while now. You two sure play bold. How about trying something new and playing around with us for a bit?"

The room was completely bare, leaving me unable to find a single object to defend myself with.

Gritting my teeth, I turned to sprint out the door, only to be instantly caught by the men. They yanked hard on my hair and slammed me violently onto the floor.

"This chick's got a smooth body. We've had our eyes on you for ages, and we finally got our chance today."

As he spoke, he lunged forward, pinning me firmly against the ground.

I struggled with everything I had, letting out desperate, piercing screams. Just as his rough hands were about to reach my clothes, the apartment door was suddenly kicked wide open.

Immediately after, the man pinning me down was sent flying across the floor by a powerful force.

Seeing their buddy get hit, the remaining two men prepared to charge forward to assist.

"I am the main investor for this development. Do you really dare to raise a hand?"

The three workers exchanged uneasy looks, as if trying to weigh whether those words were real or a bluff.

After a few seconds of hesitation, they spat out a curse and frantically scrambled out the door.

Julian unbuttoned his suit jacket, draped it over my trembling body, and turned to instruct his assistant beside him. "Track those men down. Deal with them however you see fit."

The assistant nodded and turned to head outside.

Only then did Julian bend down to look at me. "Do you remember me now?"

My hands violently yanked the suit jacket off, my face drained of all color.

"I don't."

I shoved the jacket back to him and picked up my suitcase. "Thank you for your help today."

Just as I tried to step past him, Julian suddenly grabbed hold of my wrist. "You seem terribly afraid of me."

"We don't even know each other, so why would I be afraid of you?"

"Fair point." Julian let go of my wrist. "What are you doing here anyway? If I'm not mistaken, this building is far from finished."

"None of your business."

"That's not entirely true. I happen to be the investor behind this whole project."

Julian stared at me intently. "Tell me, what would happen if I sued Jax for illegal entry and squatting?"

"What on earth do you actually want?"

"I want you." Julian didn't bother hiding his intentions in the slightest. "Chloe, I made my goal clear right from the start—my target is you."

"And I made myself clear as well. I'm a married woman."

"That doesn't matter to me at all."

I bit my lip tightly. He bent down until his eyes were level with mine. "That man can't give you the prosperous life you truly deserve. A precious gem like you shouldn't be ruined in his hands. Make your choice: do you want to see him rot in prison, or do you want to live a life of luxury by my side?"

I gripped the handle of my suitcase tightly, turned around, and walked away without looking back.

This time, Julian made no move to stop me.

Only after stepping completely out of the residential complex did I realize that my entire body was shaking violently.

Three male workers had followed me right into the room, and then Julian had conveniently appeared at the exact right moment like a knight in shining armor.

There was no way on earth I would ever believe this was just a coincidence.

Even after being reborn into this life, Julian hadn't changed at all.

He was still the kind of ruthless man who would resort to any dirty trick to get whatever he wanted.

I called Jax frantically, but the line produced nothing except endless, unanswered rings.

As dusk began to settle, a sense of restless anxiety swelled deep in my heart.

Ever since we got married, Jax had never come home this late.

I decided to run straight to the auto repair shop where he worked.

The scene before my eyes was complete chaos, with tools and equipment scattered everywhere.

I hurriedly grabbed hold of a worker who was cleaning up the mess. "Sir, do you know where Jax went?"

"Jax? He went to the hospital."

My heart sank instantly. "Why did he go to the hospital?"

The man shook his head with a heavy sigh. "Just plain bad luck. Ran into a bunch of reckless thugs who think they own the world."

After getting the exact address, I ran toward the hospital as fast as my legs could carry me. The moment I arrived, I saw Jax being wheeled out of the emergency room.

Two streaks of blood stained his face, and his right arm was wrapped in a stark white plaster cast.

His clothes were covered in dirt and speckled with bright red spots.

Tears instantly spilled from my eyes, completely uncontrollable.

"Jax!"

"Why are you here?"

He raised his remaining good arm to wipe away my tears. "Alright now, I'm not dead yet, so stop crying like it's a funeral."

Even in a state like this, he still had the heart to joke around.

Just as I was about to scold him, a voice suddenly chimed in from right behind us.

"Damn, Boss, my sister-in-law is drop-dead gorgeous! She looks just like a fairy that descended to earth!"

The young guy then scratched his head and muttered, "No wonder you sneak off home early every single night, Boss. If I had a wife this pretty, I wouldn't bother stepping out on the street either."

Only then did I notice the young man standing right behind Jax.

He looked quite young, staring at me with bright, innocent eyes.

His blunt compliment made me feel a bit embarrassed, while Jax curved his lips into a smug grin. "Stay away from my wife."

Chapter 5

Only after asking the doctor did I learn that aside from a minor fracture in his right arm, his other injuries were merely soft tissue bruises, allowing me to finally breathe a massive sigh of relief.

"How could you be so reckless and get yourself hurt like this?"

Before Jax could speak, the young guy couldn't hold back his indignation any longer. "We ran into some arrogant rich kid today! His car didn't even have a single scratch, but he insisted that Boss broke it and demanded compensation. We refused to give in, so he brought a whole gang over to smash up the shop!"

"Why didn't you guys call the police?"

"How could we?" the young guy vented, shaking his head. "You could tell at a glance he was someone powerful. The license plate was packed with straight eights—without real power and status, where on earth could anyone score a plate like that?"

A license plate full of eights?

I froze in my tracks. "What kind of car was it?"

"A black Hummer."

The young guy sighed heavily, clasping his hands together. "What are we supposed to do when we run into a spoiled brat like that? Hey Boss, do you think the shop owner is gonna make you pay for the damage?"

Not a single word that followed managed to register in my head.

My vision seemed to turn pitch black for a split second.

It was Julian again.

Suddenly, a hand reached out and gently pinched my cheek.

I let out a soft cry of pain, meeting Jax's teasing gaze the moment I looked up.

Seeing his unbothered expression, I asked in utter confusion, "Aren't you worried at all?"

"Worried about what?"

"Well... about what comes next."

Jax let out a cold scoff. "So I quit. What can he possibly do to me?"

The corner of his lips tugged up into a cheeky grin. "Besides, I've got a wife to support me, don't I? Three grand a month, and you keep all the earnings—isn't that right?"

He was bringing up my proposal line from back then.

My face instantly turned as red as a ripe tomato.

I gave a quiet nod. "I'll give you every penny I have."

Jax flicked my forehead lightly. "Dummy."

Jax was fired.

The auto shop owner called with a tone filled with deep guilt. "I'm truly sorry about this, man. But the party backing him is just too powerful; I really can't afford to take him on..."

Jax took a drag from his cigarette as he listened, his expression showing not a single trace of emotion.

After hanging up, he turned to call out to me, "Wife."

Assuming he was feeling down over losing his job, I was quickly trying to think of comforting words.

To my surprise, he blurted out, "I think I smell terrible. Wash me up, will you?"

I blinked in utter shock. "Huh?"

When I raised my head to look at him, there wasn't a single trace of sadness on Jax's face.

And now he was asking me to give him a bath...

My face burned with intense heat. "Can't you think about anything else besides that stuff every single day?"

"Besides what?" Jax raised an eyebrow. "My right arm is in a cast and can't touch water, so I'm just asking for a little help washing up."

He then leaned close to my ear and whispered softly, "Or did your mind wander somewhere else? Feel free to spill it out. Whatever you want, I'll go along with it—even if it totally ruins this arm of mine!"

Feeling both embarrassed and annoyed, I completely forgot all about him getting fired and turned around, walking straight into the bathroom.

His loud, mischievous laughter echoed behind me.

However, while helping him bathe, Jax still managed to spot something unusual on my body.

He gently turned my neck around, his brows furrowing tight. "Where did this mark come from?"

Only then did I remember with a start that there was a bloody scratch on my neck.

There were even a few faint red bruises that looked like marks left behind by someone grabbing me forcefully.

They were probably the result of the struggle earlier that day.

I quickly let down my hair to cover it up. "I was just careless."

Jax let out a cold laugh. "So careless that you managed to choke yourself and scratch your own neck along the way?"

Having my lie completely exposed, I had no choice but to tell him the truth.

"When I went back to the apartment building to grab our things earlier, a few guys followed me inside."

Seeing Jax's expression darken instantly, I hurriedly clarified, "But nothing terrible actually happened! Someone showed up just in time to rescue me."

Jax remained dead silent.

The two of us stood quietly inside the cramped space.

The steam inside the bathroom slowly dispersed, and a chilly draft seeped through the cracks, making me shiver slightly.

Only then did Jax snap back to reality, quickly grabbing a large bath towel to wrap tightly around me.

I tugged on his hand. "Are you mad?"

"I shouldn't have let you go there all by yourself."

His eyes were filled with raw pain and unhidden regret.

But I was the one at fault.

Unable to hold it back any longer, I spilled every single detail I knew. "It was all orchestrated by Julian! He was the man who sent those roses to me at school. That Hummer belonged to him, and he smashed up your shop on purpose just to make things hard for you. Those workers were arranged by him to wait there so he could play the hero, and he even knew where we lived and threatened to sue you..."

"I don't understand why he's so obsessed with harassing me. I'm sorry, it's all my fault..."

I couldn't finish my sentence as tears poured down my face like relentless rain.

I wanted to cry out loud to release all the suppressed grievances I had carried across two lifespans.

I genuinely couldn't understand—I had never provoked Julian in any way.

Why was he so determined to never let me go?

Seeing me cry so heartbreakingly, Jax picked up a towel and gently wiped away my tears. "Hey now, why are you crying like this?"

"Aren't you going to blame me?"

"Blame you for what? Blame you for being too pretty, too attractive, so bad guys keep eyeing you?"

This guy...

The intense anguish that had just welled up inside me was completely shattered by Jax's two simple lines. I sniffled softly. "Jax, do you regret it?"

"Regret what?"

"Regret marrying me."

Jax didn't answer right away, choosing to ask a question instead. "What about you? Do you regret it? Following a penniless auto mechanic who just lost his only way to make a living?"

I shook my head furiously.

"If my wife doesn't regret it, then what on earth do I have to regret?"

Jax raised an eyebrow. "My wife passed up a rich young heir just to choose me, willing to suffer through hard times by my side. What more could I possibly ask for?"

Hearing him say that, tears threatened to spill from my eyes once again.

Except this time, it was from deep emotion.

I snuggled deep into Jax's chest. "I love you."

Jax hadn't expected me to confess so suddenly, and his entire body went rigid.

He wrapped his arms tightly around me, his voice low and raspy. "Yeah. I love you too."

Chapter 6

After that day, Jax became exceptionally busy.

Even though he had lost his job at the repair shop, he still left early and returned late every single day.

I had no idea what he was so caught up with.

He seemed equally deeply concerned about Julian's underhanded tricks.

Even after we moved back to the apartment near my school, he adamantly insisted on personally dropping me off and picking me up from work every day.

"When you're at school, make sure to walk around with your colleagues. Don't go wandering off alone, got it?"

I didn't know whether to laugh or cry. "I'm not a kindergarten kid; you don't need to worry so much."

Jax chuckled softly. "Well, to me, you really are just like a kid."

During this period, Jax seemed to have a lot more social gatherings to attend.

On occasions when he couldn't make it back in time to pick me up, he asked the young guy we met at the hospital to handle the task instead.

Feeling awkward about it, I told the young man, "It's such a short walk, I can manage on my own. You really don't need to trouble yourself next time."

"No can do, sister-in-law!" He flashed a bright grin. "I have to execute whatever task Boss gives me to perfection. Besides, a fairy like you needs proper protection!"

Failing to convince him, I turned to nag Jax instead. "If you keep doing this, people are going to call you a crazy, obsessed husband."

To my surprise, Jax didn't feel a shred of shame, proudly tilting his chin up instead. "I like spoiling my wife—who's gonna stop me?"

His attitude left me both amused and utterly helpless.

One day at school, Violet pulled me into a secluded corner again and pulled out her phone.

"Is this your husband?"

I glanced at the screen. It was a photo of Jax raising a glass to drink with a scantily clad woman.

Around Jax sat four or five other men surrounding her.

The shot was taken inside a dim bar, so his facial features weren't entirely sharp.

Yet it was unmistakably Jax.

"A friend of mine snapped this at a bar. Thought it looked like your husband, so she sent it over to me."

Violet looked at me with a mocking glint in her eyes. "I heard he got fired. In the end, he's just a street thug idling around society. Look at him now—he's fallen so low he has to work as a host pouring drinks for women! Is this the true love you're so proud of? I suggest you take a good look at yourself before you catch some disease."

"Have you said enough?"

My brow furrowed tightly. "Violet, time and time again you slander my husband, spy on him, take secret photos, and try to egg me on to get a divorce. You know full well what dirty motives you hold in your heart. I know exactly what benefits Julian has given you, even if I haven't called it out. From now on, don't ever come looking for me unless it's strictly about work."

With that, I turned and walked straight into the office.

A moment later, Violet stepped inside, her eyes rimmed with red.

I faintly overheard a few colleagues huddling around to comfort her, implying that I was being completely ungrateful.

That night, Jax came home very late.

"You're back?"

"Yeah."

I leaned in close and took a sniff. "How much booze did you drink?"

Jax deliberately rubbed his cheek against my face. "Think I smell bad?"

I laughed and dodged away. Jax immediately pulled me into a tight embrace, letting out a long sigh of relief. "Things have been tough on you lately."

I hugged him back, shaking my head softly.

The next morning, I caught the faint whiff of an unfamiliar perfume on Jax's shirt.

Near the sleeve cuff, there was even a faint, lingering lipstick stain.

Thinking back to the photo Violet had shown me yesterday, a surge of bitterness welled up in my heart.

If given a choice, I would rather pray that Jax remained just an ordinary auto mechanic.

Yet life kept moving forward, until one day I unexpectedly bumped into Jax walking alongside another woman.

That afternoon, I had gone shopping for school supplies with a few fellow teachers.

As we passed by a high-end Western restaurant, a colleague suddenly yanked my arm, asking in disbelief, "Chloe, doesn't that man over there look just like your husband?"

I turned my head to look. The man sitting right by the window was indeed Jax.

Seated across from him was an exquisitely dressed, heavily made-up woman.

My heart sank instantly.

It wasn't just someone who looked like him—it was Jax.

My face darkened. Realizing something was wrong, my colleague forced an awkward smile to smooth things over. "It's probably just a business discussion. They seem to be talking very politely."

I gave a faint nod, not wanting to watch any longer. "Let's go."

Just as I turned around to leave, my colleague suddenly let out a loud gasp and grabbed hold of me tightly. "Chloe, your husband... your husband..."

My heart hammered wildly against my ribs as I heard her continue, "He's hitting that woman!"

I blinked in shock. "What?"

Hurriedly turning my head back, I saw the elegant dining table from earlier knocked completely flat on the floor by Jax's decisive kick.

He stood tall and imposing, staring down with utter contempt at the woman whose face had drained of all color.

I rushed over quickly, catching Jax's sharp voice right as he spoke. "A woman with your looks actually thinks you can hit on me? Keep dreaming!"

With that, he flicked his jacket and turned to leave, his eyes accidentally meeting mine.

The man who had looked as fierce as a young lion just a split second prior instantly deflated the moment he saw me.

"Wife... you're here?"

He furrowed his brows, frantically trying to explain, "I didn't hit a woman, alright? What happened just now was an accident. My legs are just too long, so I accidentally bumped into the table and knocked it over!"

I let out an unexpected burst of laughter. "I'm not blaming you."

Jax let out a massive sigh of relief, grinning as he grabbed my hand tightly. "Shall we head home?"

Chapter 7

Only after returning home did I learn that the woman Jax had been interacting with recently was actually Julian's older sister.

"The whole business partnership was just a front; my real goal was to get close to his sister."

Jax let out a cold laugh. "Gemma Vance is a wild, decadent woman who loves younger guys and has done plenty of shady things under the rug. I got close to her recently, gathered a mountain of valuable dirt, and sold every bit of it off to their family group's main rivals. The news should hit the papers tomorrow morning. Even if it doesn't completely destroy them, it'll leave the Vance family in utter chaos. While they're busy cleaning up their own messy backyard, Julian won't have any time left to bother you."

"You did all of this just so Julian wouldn't be able to harass me?"

"I have to take a trip to San Jose in a few days. If I don't completely block off Julian's paths, I won't be able to rest easy."

Hearing that Jax was leaving soon, a deep wave of reluctance welled up in my heart.

"Why are you suddenly going on a business trip? How long will you be gone?"

"About a week. If things go smoothly this time, I might have to travel back and forth pretty often in the future."

And just like that, Jax became immensely busy with his newly established logistics company.

"During my time as a mechanic, I built up quite a few connections and managed to rent a couple of delivery trucks. Since everything is brand new, I have to handle things personally for now."

He stroked my hair softly. "Wait for me to come back."

On the day Jax set off, I quietly slipped my bank card into his pocket.

Inside was my entire life savings of thirty thousand dollars, accumulated over many years of hard work.

It was literally every single cent I owned.

I left a small note behind: "You can't go far without cash on you. The password is our wedding date. Hurry back home safe and sound."

While Jax was away, the Vance family's scandal indeed dominated the front-page news.

Because someone was pulling the strings from behind the scenes, their dark secrets were dragged into the light, causing a massive public uproar.

Overwhelmed with trouble, Julian truly had no time left to show up and bother me.

My life gradually returned to a peaceful routine.

As the end of the term approached, the school began voting for the outstanding teacher award.

There was only a single slot for the entire school.

Based on the initial evaluations, the school had originally planned to nominate me.

Yet when the official list was announced, the person selected turned out to be Violet.

The administration was caught in an awkward position and had to invite me in for a chat, explaining that it was a direct order from higher-ups.

Everyone involved understood the true reason behind it crystal clear.

Stepping out of the principal's office, I bumped straight into Violet.

Ever since the incident with the photo, we had barely spoken a word to each other.

Violet lifted her chin, let out a faint scoff, and brushed past me.

Initially, I didn't care much about the matter.

Yet that night, during a video call with Jax, a sudden wave of helplessness flooded over me.

I pouted, desperately trying to swallow back my tears.

Jax picked up on my unusual mood instantly. "What's wrong?"

Not wanting him to worry, I made up an excuse. "Nothing really. I just watched a super emotional movie, so my chest feels a bit tight."

Jax burst into laughter. "Dummy."

After hanging up, I gave myself a gentle slap on the cheek.

I mocked myself for growing weaker the longer I lived.

In my past life, when my father was wrongfully imprisoned and my mother fell gravely ill and passed away, I had gritted my teeth without shedding a single tear.

Yet now, over such a minor setback, I was on the verge of crying right in front of Jax.

In the quiet hours of the early morning, while I was drifting in a light sleep, I suddenly heard the sound of a key turning in the lock.

My heart skipped a terrified beat.

Fearing Julian had come to cause trouble, I hurriedly grabbed a baseball bat to defend myself, holding my breath behind the door as I waited.

The moment the door creaked open, I swung the bat outward with all my strength.

As if expecting it, the intruder reacted with lightning speed, catching the bat firmly before a familiar teasing voice echoed. "What's the matter? Hiding a man at home and trying to murder your husband?"

I gasped in sheer joy. "Hubby! Why are you back at this hour?"

"My wife isn't happy, so her husband had to catch an express flight back to coax her, didn't he?"

"How did you know I was unhappy?"

"How could I not know? Your acting skills can't fool my eyes."

All the grievances I had suppressed instantly burst forth in a stream of tears.

Jax let out a helpless sigh. "You really are a total crybaby."

Jax stayed for just one night, heading back to San Jose early the next morning.

Seeing how reluctant I was to part, he leaned down and kissed my forehead gently. "The registration procedures for the company in San Jose are all wrapped up. Come join me there once summer break starts!"

I nodded repeatedly, beginning to count down the days.

Half a month later, the students officially entered summer break.

Without a moment's hesitation, I bought a direct flight straight to San Jose.

Only when Jax drove me to the company headquarters was I left completely dumbfounded, realizing the reality was vastly different from what I had imagined.

I had assumed Jax had merely opened a small delivery outfit, renting a few trucks to transport goods.

Yet right before my eyes stood an integrated logistics corporate group, combining e-commerce, transportation, and express delivery services.

Though its scale wasn't massive yet, it was already handling numerous vital supply chains.

I was so stunned I couldn't utter a word. "You... how did you come up with this model?"

I remembered clearly that in my past life, Jax hadn't gone to San Jose at all.

He had started as an ordinary mechanic, later discovered by the owner of a foreign trade enterprise and recruited into an international automotive group.

Only later did he branch out to form his own chip research team, securing his very first rounds of capital.

Gradually, his automotive chip business grew exponentially.

Yet now, his life trajectory had completely shifted because of my appearance.

He had left the repair shop earlier, missing the chance encounter with his former employer.

He had come to San Jose and built his empire from scratch in the logistics industry.

"E-commerce in San Jose has been booming like crazy these past few years, while the logistics sector was facing a massive shortage. It just so happened I knew my way around vehicles, so I pooled all my capital to take a shot here."

In reality, there was one thing Jax hadn't mentioned to me. Even though we used to live in the same city, the most powerful force there still belonged to the Vance family's group.

If Jax had started his business there, he would have faced relentless interference and sabotage.

For my sake, he was willing to leave everything behind to move to a completely unfamiliar city.

Chapter 8

My heart ached for him, yet I was equally awed by his sharp business acumen.

I was absolute certain that the logistics group Jax founded would become an indispensable link in San Jose's e-commerce ecosystem.

Jax had already rented an apartment in advance.

It was a lovely two-bedroom unit.

The layout was comparable to our place back home, but the rent was three times as expensive.

"Living expenses are steep here, and I poured all my savings into the company," Jax said, scratching his head with a rare trace of bashfulness in his voice. "The money on your card... I ended up spending a bit of it."

"That was your money to begin with."

Jax placed a soft kiss on my lips. "From now on, my entire salary will be transferred straight into your account."

With the company up and running, he couldn't keep traveling back and forth between two cities forever.

I myself held no lingering attachment to our old city.

After discussing it with Jax, I decided to resign from my job so I could move to San Jose to build our future together.

When the new school term approached, I returned to my old school to complete the resignation paperwork.

By this time, Jax's logistics company had established a firm foothold, and he was even invited for a television interview as a promising young entrepreneur.

The colleagues who had once bragged about their husbands driving Mercedes and asked Jax to service their cars rushed over to greet me the moment they saw me. "I saw your husband on TV the other day! You really have an eye for men, picking such a high-potential husband. Look at you living the good life now!"

Another person immediately chimed in, "Tell me about it! Chloe's judgment is top-tier. If you have any outstanding male friends, make sure to introduce them to us!"

I swept a cold glance across the group crowding around me so eagerly.

They were the exact same faces that had once whispered mockingly behind Jax's back.

Not wanting to waste another breath, I simply offered a polite, faint smile to get it over with.

"Chloe Crawford."

Just as I reached the school gates, someone called out my name.

It was Violet.

Dressed in a black professional suit, she hurried toward me.

Her tone sounded somewhat stiff. "I stood up to matchmake you with my cousin back then for a bit of petty gain. I owe you an apology."

I bit my lip. "You don't need to apologize to me."

"Wait!"

Violet suddenly grabbed my hand. "Chloe, I'm sorry, I really had no choice..."

The moment she finished her sentence, my head spun wildly, and I plummeted into a void of darkness.

When I woke up, I found myself lying on a massive bed.

The mattress was plush and soft.

The room was immensely vast, and outside the floor-to-ceiling windows lay a vibrant garden filled with blooming roses.

This was... the exact mansion where I had been imprisoned for ten whole years.

My brain scrambled in an instant, unable to tell whether this was real or a dream spanning two lifespans.

Could it be... I had never died at all?

Was I still stuck in this mansion, and everything that happened before was just a long, elaborate dream?

Just as I was caught in panic and confusion, the bedroom door was suddenly pushed open.

Julian stepped inside.

"You're awake?"

Instinctively, I tried to sit up, only to find my entire body completely limp and devoid of strength.

"Don't waste your energy struggling; you can't move."

Thinking back to Violet right before I passed out, I asked with pure hatred, "Did you do this? Julian, do you realize that illegal confinement is against the law?"

The moment the words left my mouth, Julian burst into a bitter laugh, as if he had just heard a ridiculous joke.

He laughed like a lunatic.

"Illegal confinement? Chloe Crawford, you belonged to me in the first place!"

He stepped closer, a wild frenzy glinting in his eyes. "Don't you know? We were meant to be together! We've been living right here in this house all along!"

It was in that exact moment that the truth finally struck me.

Why no matter how carefully I tried to hide, I could never escape Julian's reach.

It turned out he was reborn as well.

I instantly regained my composure. "Meant to be together? Julian, every single day spent by your side felt like a living nightmare—a fate worse than death. If you hadn't used my parents to threaten me, why on earth would I have ever stayed in this godforsaken place?"

Hearing this, clear shock washed over Julian's face. "You..."

"That's right. I remember everything from my past life, too."

I stared straight into Julian's eyes. "So in this life, I will absolutely never give you another chance to hurt me again."

"How could I ever bring myself to hurt you? No, that's not right... Chloe, you loved me! We were together for ten whole years! It was my fault—I shouldn't have left you alone in this mansion, or else you wouldn't have..."

He paused abruptly.

Yet I knew all too well what he wanted to say. If he hadn't, I wouldn't have died.

I let out a sneer of utter disgust. "Julian, do you still not get it? After my parents passed away, why do you think I endured staying by your side? It was because I wanted to gather evidence to avenge them! I wanted to see you thrown behind bars with my own eyes—otherwise, how could I ever rest in peace?"

"Don't you want to know why your corporate secrets kept leaking out? Why those filthy, illegal deals you hid so carefully ended up in the hands of the police? It was all my doing! My sole purpose was to make you pay in blood!"

"Enough!"

"It's far from enough!" I decided to release every ounce of built-up fury. "After you were arrested, I was the one who set fire to this entire mansion! I wanted you to lose everything!"

"Including the thing you defiled."

"Which was myself."

"Why... I loved you so much, why..."

In my past life, I had rejected Julian's confession.

He had immediately framed my father, pushing him into predatory loan traps to force me into submission. My father, refusing to let his daughter be forced into marriage, willingly turned himself in.

My mother collapsed and fell gravely ill, and Julian cut off her medication supply, forcing me to shut my eyes and move into his mansion to live with him.

I looked at Julian with ice-cold eyes. "Love? Do you even deserve to utter that word?"

With that, I sat straight up from the bed and untied the bindings on my wrists.

Julian stared in utter disbelief. "How are you..."

"How can I move, right?" I looked at him coldly. "In our past life, you used drugs to harm me. Do you really think I'd fall for the exact same trick twice?"

Saying that, I raised my hand and signaled toward the door.

The police officers waiting in ambush outside immediately burst inside.

Julian went completely mad, screaming at me hysterically, "Chloe, let's erase the past and start all over, alright? I don't care about anything you did—I forgive you for all of it!"

I caught sight of Jax's figure slowly emerging at the doorway.

I picked up my pace, running straight toward him.

As I brushed past Julian, I leaned close to his ear and whispered softly, "Too bad I will never forgive you."

Julian was led away in handcuffs by the police.

My entire body relaxed, and I collapsed into Jax's warm embrace.

"Hubby, don't you have anything you want to ask me?"

Jax shook his head gently. "It's all in the past now."

He was right—everything was officially in the past.

All the debts, rights, and wrongs of the past no longer had anything to do with me.

From now on, I was simply Jax's wife.

"When you have time, take me to visit my parents' graves, okay?"

The corner of Jax's mouth curved upward as he softly replied, "Sure."

From the very first day I was reborn, I had found an excuse to persuade my parents to leave our old city.

They had moved back to their hometown to retire early, enjoying a peaceful life.

My parents had no idea I was already married to Jax, and I hadn't dared to blurt it out so abruptly.

It wasn't until the following Spring Festival that Jax brought a generous stack of gifts to my family's house to pay his respects, taking the opportunity to formally ask for my hand in marriage.

By March of the following year, we officially walked down the aisle hand in hand.

Having lived through two lifespans, this was the very first time I wore a gorgeous wedding dress.

Jax's parents had passed away early, and he had lived without relatives since he was young.

The groom's side was attended solely by my family to share in the joy.

When he respectfully bowed, poured tea, and called out "Mom, Dad," I clearly saw his eyes turn red with tears.

Involuntarily, my thoughts drifted back to my past life.

When I passed away back then, Jax was already in his nineties, yet he remained completely single.

I had never heard a single rumor regarding his romantic life.

He lived in solitude, no different from an outsider to the mortal world.

"If you hadn't married me, what kind of wife were you planning to find?"

On our wedding night, I couldn't help turning over to ask Jax.

"Who knows?" Jax pondered for a moment. "Probably wouldn't have looked for anyone. Got used to being alone."

"Then why did you agree to my proposal back then?"

Hearing that, Jax seemed to reminisce about the past.

As if recalling something, he flashed a mischievous grin.

He said, "Back then, I just thought having an extra person around might be nice. Especially when that bride happened to be as pretty as you."

My eyes widened. "So you actually said yes just because you were seduced by my looks?"

"Not entirely. After all, you had that bank card too."

Jax held back a laugh. "I get pretty swayed by money, you know."

Seeing me pout in playful annoyance, Jax leaned down and kissed my forehead gently. "Thank you, sweetheart, for giving me a real family."

No, that wasn't right.

The one who truly needed to say thank you was me.

Thank you to the heavens for giving me the chance to be reborn, so I could find the true love of my life.
